Why Is My Grass Brown?

Diagnose the most common causes of brown lawn areas: drought, mowing stress, fertilizer burn, pet damage, disease, compaction and drainage problems.

Start with the pattern. Uniform browning across a sunny lawn often points to drought or heat stress. Stripes suggest mowing or fertilizer application. Small isolated spots can indicate pet urine, chemical damage or localized root problems.

Common causes of brown grass

Drought and heat

Established cool-season grass can brown or enter temporary dormancy during prolonged dry weather. Check soil moisture several centimetres below the surface before assuming the lawn needs fertilizer.

Mowing stress

Cutting too low, removing more than about one-third of the leaf at once, or using a blunt blade can leave brown tips and weaken turf during hot weather.

Fertilizer burn

Over-application or overlapping spreader passes may create yellow-brown streaks. Do not add more fertilizer to correct fertilizer damage.

Pet or chemical spots

Small dead areas with darker green margins may follow concentrated urine or salts. Flush recent damage with water where appropriate and reseed dead turf after the cause is removed.

Disease

Irregular patches that expand in humid conditions can be associated with fungal disease. Reduce prolonged leaf wetness and excessive nitrogen before considering treatment.

Compaction and drainage

Brown or thin turf in traffic zones or persistently wet areas may have root-zone problems. Aeration, drainage correction and soil improvement can matter more than surface feeding.

A practical diagnosis sequence

1

Check moisture

Inspect soil below the surface. Dry topsoil alone does not tell the whole story.

2

Check the damage pattern

Uniform, circular, striped and path-shaped damage point toward different causes.

3

Check mowing history

Look for torn leaf tips, scalping and a recent sudden reduction in mowing height.

4

Check recent fertilizer or chemical use

Application patterns often reveal the source of burn.

5

Repair after fixing the cause

Dead patches can be loosened and overseeded once moisture, compaction or application errors are corrected.

Do not automatically fertilize brown grass. Extra nitrogen can worsen drought stress, fertilizer injury and some disease situations.
