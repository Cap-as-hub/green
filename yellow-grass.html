Why Is My Lawn Yellow?

Use lawn colour, moisture, drainage and growth pattern to work through common causes of yellow grass before choosing a treatment.

What yellowing can mean

Too much water or poor drainage

Roots need oxygen. Persistent waterlogging can cause weak growth and yellow turf even when the surface looks well supplied with water.

Too little water

Drought stress can begin as dull or yellow-green colour before progressing to brown dormancy.

Nutrient shortage

Low nitrogen can produce generally pale turf, but nutrient symptoms should be considered together with growth conditions and, ideally, soil testing.

Compaction

Compacted soil restricts air, water movement and roots. Traffic lines are a useful clue.

Uneven fertilizer

Light and dark bands can follow spreader paths. More fertilizer is not the answer until the application pattern is understood.

Root-zone stress

Shallow roots, poor soil depth and construction compaction can cause chronic yellowing despite regular watering.

Check before you treat

  1. Push a screwdriver or soil probe into several areas to compare compaction and moisture.
  2. Compare sunny, shaded and low-lying zones.
  3. Review the last fertilizer application and spreader pattern.
  4. Check whether grass is actually growing slowly, or only losing colour.
  5. Correct water and root-zone problems before adding more nutrients.
