Diagnosis before treatment

Lawn Problem Solver

Identify likely causes of brown grass, yellowing, bare patches, moss, weeds, poor germination, uneven growth and mowing or fertilizer damage.

Choose the symptom first

Brown grass

Think drought, mowing stress, fertilizer burn, pet damage, disease, compaction or drainage.

Open brown grass guide →

Yellow grass

Think waterlogging, drought, nutrient imbalance, compaction or poor root function.

Open yellow grass guide →

Bare patches

Check traffic, pet damage, failed germination, hard soil and seed-to-soil contact.

Thin turf

Compare shade, soil fertility, compaction, water and whether the seed mix suits the site.

Moss

Moss often indicates shade, compaction, weak turf, drainage issues or unsuitable soil conditions. Check pH before deciding to lime.

Weeds

Open soil and weak turf invite weeds. Improving lawn density is part of long-term control.

Seed does not germinate

Check moisture history, planting depth, soil temperature, seed age and whether heavy watering moved the seed.

Brown tips after mowing

A blunt blade, low cut or removing too much height at once are common clues.

Four questions before taking action

  1. Did the problem appear after a weather change, mowing, fertilizing or chemical use?
  2. Is the pattern uniform, circular, striped, isolated or concentrated in traffic areas?
  3. Is the soil actually dry, waterlogged or compacted below the surface?
  4. Is the grass still alive and rooted, or is reseeding needed after the cause is fixed?
Diagnosis matters. Adding fertilizer to every weak lawn can make drought, salt damage and some disease situations worse.
